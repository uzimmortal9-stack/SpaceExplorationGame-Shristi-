Create a fully playable, polished 3D sci-fi space exploration game. It should be built either as a browser-based game or as a standalone game using a custom game engine, whichever you are best able to implement cleanly and reliably.

If building in the browser, use a modular pure JavaScript or TypeScript WebGL/WebGPU implementation with a clean project structure. If building a custom engine, use a traditional, reliable game-development language such as C++ with OpenGL, Vulkan, or DirectX, and use appropriate libraries for windowing, input, math, audio, and model loading.

Use downloaded 3D models and external image textures where useful, especially for ship interiors, props, furniture, panels, seats, tools, storage units, terrain detail, vegetation, rocks, ruins, and sci-fi equipment. If downloaded assets are not available for something, generate high-quality procedural geometry, materials, and audio instead. Keep a list of external assets and credits.

The game must feel complete, immersive, and playable from start to finish. The player should be able to explore the starship interior in first person, sit in the pilot seat, fly the ship in space, select a destination, initiate a warp jump, enter an alien planet’s atmosphere, land on the surface, exit the ship, and explore the alien jungle planet on foot. All major transitions should feel smooth, seamless, and cinematic.

## 1. Starship Interior — First-Person Exploration

### General Interior Requirements

Create a fully traversable sci-fi starship interior. The ship should feel like a believable, lived-in spacecraft used by a small crew for long-duration exploration. The interior should be modular, logically connected, and easy to navigate.

The ship must include:

- Clean first-person movement with proper collision detection.
- Fully functional automatic sliding doors between rooms.
- Door opening and closing animations with hydraulic or mechanical sound effects.
- Door obstruction safety behavior so doors do not close on the player.
- Proper walkable clearance through all doorways and corridors.
- No blocking props, floating objects, invisible walls, or clutter trapping the player.
- Smooth lighting transitions between rooms.
- Subtle sci-fi ambient hum throughout the ship.
- Functional switches, buttons, panels, seats, storage units, terminals, and interactive props wherever possible.
- Interior signage and small labels to help the player identify rooms and systems.
- A cohesive sci-fi visual style: metallic panels, glowing trim lights, utility conduits, soft blue/white interior lighting, warning accents near engineering areas, and warm lighting in crew quarters.
- Environmental storytelling: personal items, tools, notebooks, bags, photos, plants, maintenance panels, supply crates, and small decorative details.

Do not show permanent control-key hints on the screen. Show the controls only once when the player first enters the ship. After that, controls should only be accessible from a Settings panel.

## 26. Warp Jump Sequence

The warp jump should be a major cinematic moment.

The sequence should happen as follows:

### Step 1: Warp Preparation
- Player selects a target destination.
- Warp core becomes available.
- Dashboard or engine room displays warp readiness.
- Fuel requirement is checked.
- Optional safety confirmation appears.

### Step 2: Warp Core Spin-Up
- The warp core begins spinning up.
- Particle effects increase.
- Energy pipes glow brighter.
- Audio hum rises in pitch and intensity.
- Screen shake begins subtly.
- Interior lights may flicker or pulse.
- Dashboard displays charging percentage or energy buildup.

### Step 3: Lever Activation
- Player opens the red safety switch cover.
- Player pulls the physical warp lever.
- The warp tunnel activates.

### Step 4: Warp Tunnel
- Animated hyperspace distortion shader.
- Light streaks.
- Motion blur.
- Chromatic aberration if appropriate.
- Speed sense increasing rapidly.
- Audio transition from core hum to warp travel roar/whoosh.
- Camera vibration and FOV shift.

### Step 5: Warp Exit
- The ship exits warp smoothly.
- The target alien jungle planet appears ahead.
- The ship is placed into a stable orbit or approach vector around the target planet.
- Dashboard updates to show arrival complete.
- Warp core cools down.

The warp sequence should feel powerful, deliberate, and immersive.

### 1.0 ASSET PIVOT NORMALIZATION, BOUNDING BOX ALIGNMENT & GROUND CLAMPING (ZERO-FLOAT DIRECTIVE)

To eliminate common AI-generation defects (such as floating props, misaligned centers, offset pivot origins, or meshes buried halfway into surfaces), the engine must run an automated **Geometry Alignment & Surface Snapping Pipeline** for every single placed object, prop, and interior furniture piece before rendering:

1. **Automated Pivot Center & Origin Normalization**:
   - For every 3D mesh asset (procedural or imported glTF/GLB), compute its local Axis-Aligned Bounding Box (AABB) dynamically:
     ```typescript
     geometry.computeBoundingBox();
     const bbox = geometry.boundingBox;
     const size = new Vector3();
     const center = new Vector3();
     bbox.getSize(size);
     bbox.getCenter(center);
     ```
   - **Pivot Calibration Rules**:
     - **Floor-Mounted / Surface Props** (Chairs, Desks, Laptops, Cups, Beds, Trees, Monoliths, Crates): Automatically re-center the $X$ and $Z$ axes to the true bounding box center ($0.0$), but offset the local $Y$ origin to the **absolute bottom** of the mesh ($Y_{\text{origin}} = \text{bbox.min.y}$). This ensures the origin point $(0, 0, 0)$ is flush with the contact surface.
     - **Wall-Mounted Assets** (Screens, PDLC Windows, Wall Racks, Light Fixtures): Set the back surface of the bounding box to $Z_{\text{origin}} = \text{bbox.max.z}$ (or flush plane) so the asset snaps flat against the wall normal without clipping inside walls or hovering in air.
     - **Ceiling Assets & Light Panels**: Set the top surface of the bounding box to $Y_{\text{origin}} = \text{bbox.max.y}$ to guarantee seamless ceiling-recess alignment.
     - **Floating / Dynamic Kinetic Items** (Hologram, Space Dust, Spinning Warp Core): Maintain true geometric volumetric centroid:
       $$\mathbf{C} = \frac{1}{2} (\mathbf{P}_{\text{min}} + \mathbf{P}_{\text{max}})$$

2. **Automated Surface Snapping (Raycast Surface Clamp)**:
   - When placing any object on floors, desks, shelves, or planetary terrain:
     - Cast a downward vertical test ray from the spawn coordinate:
       $$\text{Ray}_{\text{snap}}(\mathbf{O}, \mathbf{D}) \quad \text{where} \quad \mathbf{D} = (0, -1, 0)$$
     - Align the bottom origin ($Y_{\text{bottom}} = 0$) directly to the raycast hit point:
       $$Y_{\text{prop}} = Y_{\text{hit\_surface}} + \epsilon \quad (\text{where } \epsilon = 0.001\text{m to prevent Z-fighting})$$
     - If the terrain has a slope, rotate the object's Up-vector $\mathbf{u}$ to align with the surface normal vector $\mathbf{n}$:
       $$\mathbf{q}_{\text{rot}} = \text{Quaternion.FromToRotation}(\mathbf{u}_{\text{up}}, \mathbf{n}_{\text{surface}})$$

3. **Collision Hull Conformity**:
   - Generate invisible simplified primitive colliders (BoxCollider, CapsuleCollider, or ConvexHull) directly from the normalized bounding box.
   - The bottom plane of the collider must perfectly match the bottom plane of the visual mesh ($Y_{\text{min\_collider}} \equiv Y_{\text{min\_mesh}}$) so the player's camera and feet physically walk on top of surfaces without floating in mid-air or sinking into geometry.


### 1.0.1 PROFESSIONAL UI/UX BENCHMARK RESEARCH & DIEGETIC INTERFACE SYSTEM (ANTI-SLOP DIRECTIVE)

Do not generate generic, placeholder, or flat "AI-slop" UI overlays (e.g., raw centered rectangles, neon blue gradients with generic sans-serif fonts, or floating clutter covering gameplay). 

Before writing any HUD or menu code, systematically align the UI architecture with professional AAA sci-fi game interface benchmarks (*Star Citizen* mobiGlas, *Elite Dangerous* holographic cockpits, *Dead Space* diegetic suit projections, and *Alien: Isolation* retro-industrial CRTs):

1. **Pre-Design Architectural Research & Benchmarking**:
   - **Cockpit Avionics**: Research modern military HUD collimation (infinity-focus projection), pitch ladders, velocity prograde/retrograde markers, and integrated MFDs (Multi-Function Displays) angled along the cockpit dashboard's physical geometry.
   - **Player Status & Menus**: Research in-world diegetic UI (wrist-mounted holographic slates, physical tablet slates held in hands, and wall-mounted tactile screens) rather than flat static 2D screen overlays.
   - **Color Palette & Visual Hierarchy**:
     * Primary Flight/Nav Data: High-contrast amber/gold (`#FFB000`) or crisp tactical cyan (`#00F0FF`) with subtle 5% additive glow bloom.
     * Caution / Warnings: High-visibility hazard orange (`#FF6600`) and emergency red (`#FF2244`).
     * Background Panels: Low-opacity dark obsidian glass (`rgba(10, 15, 22, 0.75)`) with subtle 1px brushed-metal borders (`#1F3347`) and backdrop blur (12px blur filter).

2. **Diegetic & Spatial Interface Engineering**:
   - **Zero Flat Screen Overlays in Exploration**: When walking around the ship or planetary surface, 0% of the screen is blocked by static 2D health bars or floating objective markers.
   - **Interactive World-Space UI (3D In-Engine Canvases)**:
     * Door panels, life-support terminals, reactor breakers, and coffee machines must use high-resolution world-space canvas textures rendered directly onto the 3D surface geometry with reactive hover states.
     * Pressing an interaction key snaps the player's focus smoothly toward the physical screen (subtle FOV zoom with depth-of-field background blur), maintaining full in-world immersion.
   - **Cockpit Collimated Projection**: The flight HUD must be mapped to a true parallax collimated shader—when the pilot moves their head (G-force or mouse look), the HUD reticle remains locked to target vectors in infinite space rather than sliding on the windshield glass.

3. **Typography, Layout & Micro-Interactions**:
   - **Typography System**: Use clean, monospaced or technical geometric typefaces (e.g., *Share Tech Mono*, *Orbitron*, *Rajdhani*, or *DIN Pro* equivalents) with strict typographic scale (Header: 14pt bold, Subhead: 11pt medium, Telemetry Data: 9pt mono).
   - **Dynamic Data Visualization**: All numbers (speed, fuel, altitude, shield %) must dynamically roll/tick smoothly with physics updates instead of snapping abruptly.
   - **Tactile Audio-Visual Feedback**: Every button, lever, and toggle must feature a complete 3-state tactile loop:
     1. *Hover*: Subtle 1px edge highlight + high-pitch micro-click tone (1200 Hz).
     2. *Depress*: 2mm physical mesh translation / active state color shift + heavy mechanical switch clunk.
     3. *Release/Engage*: Confirm chime / animated progress indicator fill.
